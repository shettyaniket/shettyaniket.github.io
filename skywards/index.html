Skywards

Skywards is a personal flight log. Type a flight number and a date and it fills in the route, times and terminals for you. Your upcoming trips sit on a 3D globe with a countdown to the next one, and every flight you've taken adds up in a logbook of distance, hours, airports and countries. No account, no ads, no subscription — your flights are stored on your device.

Every flight, in one place.

For people who like knowing where they've been, and when they're off next.

✍️ Add a flight by typing it

Write "6E5047 on 14th June" and Skywards works out the rest. You can also scan a boarding pass, import from Apple Wallet, enter it by hand or bring in a CSV of past flights.

🌍 Your trips on a 3D globe

Upcoming routes are drawn on a globe you can spin and zoom, focused on where your next flight leaves from. Switch to a flat map whenever you prefer.

⏳ A countdown to the next one

The next flight sits at the top as a boarding-pass card — route, local times, terminals, weather at both ends and the days left to go. Connecting legs are grouped with their layovers.

📖 A logbook that adds up

Distance flown, hours in the air, airports, countries, airlines and aircraft types, by year or all time, with charts and your personal records.

🎬 Your year as a video

Turn a year of flying into a short replay video with your routes animated on the map, ready to save or share.

🔔 Reminders before you fly

Web check-in, passport, power bank, departure alerts — pick what you want and when, with separate sets for personal and work trips. Add any flight to your calendar in a tap.

🎨 Make it yours

Six colour themes, kilometres or miles, your date format, and a home airport so outbound and return legs are told apart.

🔒 Free and private

No account, no ads, no analytics. Your flight history stays on your device; the app only goes online to look up flight details, weather and maps.

A note on flight details

Routes and times come from public flight-data sources and are a convenience, not an official record. Airlines change schedules, gates and terminals — always confirm with your airline before you travel. If a lookup gets something wrong, you can try another source or edit the flight yourself.
