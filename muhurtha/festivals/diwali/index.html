When it falls

New moon of Ashwina. Like every lunar festival it lands on a different Gregorian date each year. Muhurtha works the date out from the positions of the Sun and Moon, using the rule for this festival; see how every festival is dated.

Significance

Diwali, or Deepavali, is the most widely kept festival in India — a celebration of light over darkness and knowledge over ignorance. For most Hindus it is the night Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth and fortune, is welcomed into the home; much of the North also remembers Rama's return to Ayodhya after fourteen years of exile. For Gujarati and many trading communities it opens the new financial year.

The story

The darkest night of the year, lit up. Rama returned to Ayodhya; Lakshmi rose from the ocean; Krishna's Narakasura victory was celebrated. Diyas are set out so Lakshmi can find her way in. It's judged at twilight — Lakshmi Puja time — which occasionally makes it fall a day before the Amavasya-at-sunrise date.

Diwali sits in the middle of a five-day run — Dhanteras, then Narak Chaturdashi (Choti Diwali), the main Lakshmi Puja night, Govardhan Puja or Bali Pratipada, and finally Bhai Dooj. The North remembers Rama's return to Ayodhya after fourteen years of exile; the South makes Narak Chaturdashi and a dawn oil bath the heart of the festival.

How it is kept

Clean the house, rangoli at the door, Lakshmi-Ganesha puja after sunset, sweets, and lamps in every window. Jains mark Mahavira's nirvana; Sikhs, Bandi Chhor Divas.

The main puja is done after sunset, in the Pradosh and Nishita windows, when Lakshmi is believed to visit homes that are clean and lit. Traders open fresh account books for the new year — Chopda Pujan in Gujarat — and Lakshmi is offered kheel, batasha and lotus flowers. The lamps are left to burn through the night so she can find her way in.

Regional and community variations

In North India the festival centres on the Lakshmi Puja night and Rama's homecoming; in the South it is built around Naraka Chaturdashi and a pre-dawn oil bath, and is usually called Deepavali. Bengal and Odisha worship Kali rather than Lakshmi on the same new-moon night. Gujarat opens fresh account books (Chopda Pujan) and keeps the next day as Bestu Varas, its new year. Jains mark the nirvana of Mahavira, and Sikhs keep Bandi Chhor Divas, the release of Guru Hargobind from Gwalior fort.

Foods and prasad

Homes fill with sweets and snacks — ladoo, barfi, kaju katli, chakli, shankarpali, mathri and chivda — and Lakshmi is offered kheel (puffed rice), batasha and lotus flowers. Families prepare a festive meal and share boxes of mithai with neighbours.

What to do, what to avoid

Keep the house clean and well lit and the doorway open so Lakshmi can enter, and draw a rangoli at the threshold. Avoid quarrels, darkness and clutter on the night. Use firecrackers sparingly and with care — many families now keep a quieter, lamp-lit Diwali for the sake of children, elders, animals and the air.

Common questions

When is Diwali · Lakshmi Puja in 2026?

In 2026, Diwali · Lakshmi Puja falls on Sunday, 8 November 2026 in Mumbai. Dates for other years are in the table above.

What is the significance of Diwali?

Diwali celebrates the victory of light over darkness and good over evil. For most Hindus it is the night Lakshmi, goddess of wealth, is welcomed home; the North also remembers Rama's return to Ayodhya. It is one of the few nights nearly every Indian community keeps in some form.

Which god is worshipped on Diwali?

Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth and fortune, is worshipped on the main Diwali night, usually together with Ganesha. Bengal and Odisha worship Kali instead on the same new-moon night.

How is Lakshmi Puja done on Diwali?

The house is cleaned and lit, a rangoli is drawn at the door, and after sunset — in the Pradosh and Nishita windows — Lakshmi and Ganesha are worshipped with lamps, kheel, batasha, sweets and flowers. Traders also perform Chopda Pujan on their new account books. The lamps are then left burning through the night.

What are the five days of Diwali?

Dhanteras, then Naraka Chaturdashi (Choti Diwali), the main Lakshmi Puja night, Govardhan Puja or Bali Pratipada, and finally Bhai Dooj — five days in all.

Is Diwali a vrat or a feast?

Diwali is a feast rather than a fast. Some keep a light, sattvic diet through the day and eat only after the evening Lakshmi Puja, but there is no required fast.

Is Diwali a public holiday in India?

Yes. Diwali is a gazetted public holiday across India, and many states also close for the surrounding days such as Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj.
