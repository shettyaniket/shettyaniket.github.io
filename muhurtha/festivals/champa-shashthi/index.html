When it falls

6th day of the waxing moon, in Margashirsha. Like every lunar festival it lands on a different Gregorian date each year. Muhurtha works the date out from the positions of the Sun and Moon, using the rule for this festival; see how every festival is dated.

Significance

Champa Shashthi marks the victory of Khandoba — a warrior form of Shiva (Martanda Bhairava) worshipped as a king in Maharashtra and Karnataka — over the demons Mani and Malla. The six-day festival runs from Margashirsha Pratipada to Shashthi and is the great festival of Jejuri.

The story

Khandoba, a form of Shiva worshipped as a warrior king in Maharashtra and Karnataka, is said to have killed the demons Mani and Malla. The six-day festival, from Margashirsha Pratipada to Shashthi, marks the victory.

How it is kept

Devotees fast, offer brinjal and a mash of it (bharit), turmeric and bel leaves, and visit Jejuri, where the hill temple is lit with lamps.

Fasting and parana

Many keep the six-day fast (Shadratrotsava), ending on Shashthi; the household's ghatasthapana pot is set up for the six days and the fast broken after the Shashthi worship.

Regional and community variations

It is kept most grandly at Jejuri near Pune, Khandoba's chief hill shrine, and in the Deccan where he is a family deity; Khandoba is worshipped with turmeric (bhandara) showered in abandon.

Foods and prasad

Brinjal (vangi) and a mash of it (bharit) with bajra bhakri are the traditional naivedya, offered to Khandoba with turmeric and bel leaves.

What to do, what to avoid

Keep the six-day observance, offer brinjal bharit and turmeric, and visit Jejuri where the hill is lit with lamps and showered with bhandara; it is the warrior-god's victory festival.

Common questions

When is Champa Shashthi (Khandoba) in 2026?

In 2026, Champa Shashthi (Khandoba) falls on Tuesday, 15 December 2026 in Mumbai. Dates for other years are in the table above.

What is the significance of Champa Shashthi?

It marks Khandoba's victory over the demons Mani and Malla, the close of a six-day festival for Khandoba, a warrior form of Shiva worshipped across the Deccan.

Who is Khandoba?

A warrior form of Shiva (Martanda Bhairava or Malhari Martand), worshipped as a king and family deity in Maharashtra and Karnataka, with his chief shrine at Jejuri.

What is offered on Champa Shashthi?

Brinjal and its mash (bharit) with bajra bhakri, turmeric (bhandara) and bel leaves, offered to Khandoba during the six-day festival.
