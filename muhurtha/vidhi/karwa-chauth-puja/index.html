Karwa Chauth Puja

The day's fast, the evening katha and breaking it at moonrise

Common way — most homes

Karwa Chauth, Krishna Chaturthi of Ashwina (Amanta). The puja is in the evening and the fast ends at moonrise, which Muhurtha shows for your city.

A day's fast; about 30 minutes for the puja

Englishहिन्दीमराठीবাংলাગુજરાતીಕನ್ನಡമലയാളംଓଡ଼ିଆதமிழ்తెలుగు

Married women, and many couples together now, keep this fast for each other's long life. Sargi is eaten before dawn, the women gather in the evening for the katha, and the fast is broken after offering water to the Moon. If a mantra is hard, "Om Namah Shivaya" can stand in for any of them.

Prayers in this puja

Shubham Karoti ›Vakratunda Mahakaya ›Shuklambaradharam ›Mushikavahana ›

Get ready

The day before

  • Prepare the sargi: fruit, sweets, dry fruit and something filling, traditionally sent by the mother-in-law.
  • Buy a karwa (small spouted pot), a sieve, and mehndi if you apply it.

On the day

  • Eat the sargi before sunrise and drink water.
  • Dress in the evening, and check the moonrise time for your city in the app.

The puja

  1. Sargi before dawn

    Before sunrise, eat the sargi and drink water, then make the intention for the fast. The strict form takes no food or water until moonrise.

  2. Light the lamp

    Light the lamp and place it to the right of the deity. Fold your hands to it.

    śubhaṁ karōti kalyāṇamārōgyaṁ dhanasampadaḥ. śatrubuddhivināśāya dīpajyōtirnamō'stu tē.I bow to the flame of the lamp, which brings good fortune, well-being, health and wealth, and destroys ill will.

    Shubham Karoti ›

  3. Remember Ganesha

    Every puja begins with Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. Fold your hands and say the verse once.

    vakratuṇḍa mahākāya sūryakōṭisamaprabha. nirvighnaṁ kuru mē dēva sarvakāryēṣu sarvadā.O Ganesha of the curved trunk and mighty form, radiant as a crore suns, keep my every work free of obstacles, always.

    Vakratunda Mahakaya ›Shuklambaradharam ›Mushikavahana ›

  4. Sankalpa — state your intention

    Hold a little water, akshat and a flower in your right palm. Say your name, today's date in the Hindu calendar, and why you are doing the puja — for example, for the well-being of the family. Then let the water fall on the ground or into a plate. Tap "Make sankalpa" to read today's full sankalpa.

  5. Gather for the katha

    In the evening, sit with the other women around the thali. One reads or tells the story of Veeravati while the others listen with a little rice in hand.

  6. Pass the thali

    Pass the thalis around the circle seven times, as your family's song directs. Offer the rice to the Gauri picture and bow to the elders, giving the baya to your mother-in-law or an elder.

  7. Dhyana — invite the deity

    Close your eyes and picture the deity before you. Offer a pinch of akshat as you invite them to be present.

    ōm namaḥ śivāya. dhyāyāmi, āvāhayāmi.

  8. Pushpa — flowers

    Offer the flowers at the deity's feet, one or a few at a time.

    ōm namaḥ śivāya. puṣpāṇi samarpayāmi.

  9. Dhupa — incense

    Light the incense and wave it gently before the deity three times, clockwise. Ring the bell with your left hand if you have one.

    ōm namaḥ śivāya. dhūpam āghrāpayāmi.

  10. Deepa — the lamp

    Lift the lamp and show it to the deity, circling it clockwise three times.

    ōm namaḥ śivāya. dīpaṁ darśayāmi.

  11. Naivedya — food

    Place the food before the deity. Sprinkle a little water around it, and offer it with folded hands. Wait a moment, as if it is being received.

    ōm namaḥ śivāya. naivēdyaṁ nivēdayāmi.

  12. Moonrise — arghya

    When the Moon is up, look at it through the sieve, offer water from the karwa, then look at your husband through the sieve.

  13. Kshama prarthana — ask forgiveness

    With folded hands, ask forgiveness for anything done wrong or left out. This closes every puja.

    āvāhanaṁ na jānāmi na jānāmi visarjanam. pūjāṁ caiva na jānāmi kṣamasva paramēśvara. mantrahīnaṁ kriyāhīnaṁ bhaktihīnaṁ surēśvara. yatpūjitaṁ mayā dēva paripūrṇaṁ tadastu mē.I do not know how to invite you, nor how to send you off, nor even how to worship; forgive me, O Lord. Whatever I have offered — lacking in mantra, in action or in devotion — may it be made whole.

  14. Break the fast

    Your husband gives you the first sip of water and a bite of sweet. Then eat a light meal together. If you are unwell at any point, drink water and eat; the app's moonrise time may differ a little from what you see if clouds or buildings hide the Moon.

Fasting

Traditionally no food or water from sunrise to moonrise. Those who are pregnant, unwell, diabetic or on medicine should not fast without water; a fruit-and-water fast, or none, is entirely acceptable.

Good to do

  • Fast together, if your husband wishes to; many now do.
  • If clouds hide the Moon, go by the time shown and face its direction.

Tradition avoids

  • Do not push through dizziness or faintness; stop and drink water.
  • Do not let this become pressure on anyone; the fast is a choice.

The day is about care between two people. A shared meal at the end is as much the point as the fast.

Traditions vary. Follow your own family's way.
