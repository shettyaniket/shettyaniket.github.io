LoanYield IQ · User Guide

An honest second opinion on the flat you're about to buy

LoanYield IQ takes the handful of numbers you already know — the agreement value, the rent it would fetch, the loan you'd take — and turns them into the numbers nobody shows you: what the flat really costs once stamp duty, GST and builder charges are in, how much cash you'll burn before an under-construction flat is handed over, what the tax rules give back, and whether the whole thing beats simply investing the same cheques. Then it says, in one sentence, what it thinks you should do.

This guide walks through every screen. If you only read one section, read Three scenarios — it's the part that keeps the app honest.

iPhone & iPad · iOS 18+ Free · no account 100% offline Not financial advice
Getting started

Your first minute

On first launch the app walks you through Quick Start — a welcome page and two short screens that ask for the six things a verdict needs. If you'd rather explore first, tap Use sample deal and play with the built-in 2BHK (it carries a SAMPLE badge until you rename it).

  1. 1
    About the flat

    Name the deal (e.g. 2BHK, Hinjewadi), pick the state, say whether it's ready or under construction (and months until handover), and type the flat price and the rent it would fetch. Money boxes have ₹ / L / Cr chips; tapping a box clears it so you can type straight away.

  2. 2
    About the loan

    Down payment %, interest rate and loan tenure. The loan amount is shown as you go.

  3. 3
    See my verdict

    The deal is saved and you land on its verdict. Everything — stamp-duty details, builder charges, prepayments, tax slab — can be changed later from the deal screen, and every change recomputes live.

The same Quick Start runs whenever you choose New deal from the Deals menu. To rename a deal, just tap its name above the verdict and type.

💡
Every ⓘ is a plain-English explanation

Next to nearly every label and number there's a small ⓘ. Tap it for a two-line explanation of what the term means and why it matters — PLC, MODT, Section 24(b), XIRR, all of it.

The Studio

The verdict

The card at the top of the Studio is the whole app in one glance. It gives one of three calls:

● Buy ● Marginal ● Rent + SIP instead

Under the call is one sentence explaining it — the annual return, how it compares to a Nifty SIP, how much cash you need to park before handover, and the month from which selling would return your money. Below that are the three numbers that drive the call:

DriverWhat it isGreen whenRed when
Return / yrYour 10-year leveraged XIRR — every cheque out, every rupee of rent in, and the flat's value less the loan at the end.At or above the Nifty SIPBelow the 8.5% prepayment hurdle
Yield vs loanRent after vacancy and property tax, divided by the all-in cost, judged against your loan rate.≥ 75% of the loan rate< 40% of the loan rate
Cash neededPre-EMI plus property tax during construction (or day-one cash for a ready flat), relative to your day-one cheque.≤ 10% of day-one cash> 30% of day-one cash
🎨
Colours mean judgement, not sign

A 2.8% rent yield is a positive number, but it's shown red because your loan costs 8.5% — the flat loses money every month it's rented. Everywhere in the app, green / amber / red answer "is this good?", never "is this above zero?".

The Studio

Three scenarios

The single assumption that swings a property decision more than any other is how fast prices grow. So the verdict is never shown once — it's shown three times, and each pill is coloured by its own answer:

PillPrice growth usedRead it as
CautiousYour assumption − 2.5% (never below 2%)"What if the market goes sideways?"
BaseYour assumption (default 6.5%)The headline verdict
HopefulYour assumption + 2.5%"What if the builder's brochure is right?"

Tap any pill to swap the headline, drivers and break-even for that scenario. Underneath, a one-line summary says what the decision hinges on — for the sample deal: "Marginal at 9%+ price growth; a clear pass below that."

⚠️
If it only turns green under Hopeful

You're betting on the market, not the flat. The app will say so. Change the Base assumption under Property › More › Price growth per year if you have a better local number.

The Studio

The chart

Ten years, month by month. Two things are drawn on one canvas:

  • Cash flow (left axis) — rent in, minus EMI, property tax and income tax. Red below zero, green above. The shaded block at the start is the pre-EMI period before handover.
  • Loan balance (right axis, dashed blue) — what you still owe. It steps up at handover when the full loan is disbursed and steps down at every lump-sum prepayment (marked with a green diamond).

Two vertical markers: Handover, and Break-even — the first month from which selling the flat would return everything you've paid in. Drag a finger across the chart to read any month's cash flow and outstanding loan.

💡
Lump sums are not on the cash line

A ₹5 L prepayment would dwarf every monthly bar, so the cash line shows recurring cash only. Prepayments are shown as diamonds on the loan curve, and they are included in the return and break-even calculations.

The Studio

Step-up & prepayments

Raise EMI 10% every year

One switch, off by default so your first verdict assumes nothing beyond what the bank offers. When it's on, each year's EMI is 10% higher than the last (compounding), which typically closes a 20-year loan in nine or ten years. The line under the switch shows what it saves — or, while it's off, what turning it on would save.

Lump-sum prepayment

Tap Add a lump-sum prepayment, pick a loan month and an amount. The loan curve steps down at that month and the verdict recomputes. You can add as many as you like — the quick control edits one month at a time; the full schedule lets you edit every month.

The month-by-month schedule

Tap Schedule on the Home loan card (or the table icon in the toolbar). Every month of the loan is listed: opening balance, EMI, interest, prepayment and closing balance. Tap the “+” in the Prepay column on any row to enter a lump sum for that month — type 5L, 2.5L or 5,00,000. Rows with a prepayment are highlighted green.

💡
Loan months vs. calendar months

Prepayment months count from the first full EMI, i.e. from handover. On an under-construction flat with 18 months to handover, "month 18" of the loan is month 36 on the chart.

Import a statement

My Loans → + → Import from a statement. Pick a PDF or image from Files, a photo or screenshot from Photos, or scan the paper with the camera (multiple pages are fine).

  1. 1
    The text is read on your phone

    PDFs with selectable text are read directly; scans and photos go through on-device OCR.

  2. 2
    The loan details are picked out

    Loan amount, outstanding balance, interest rate, tenure, EMI, first EMI date and lender — by Apple's on-device model where available (or your own cloud key if you added one), with a pattern-matcher as backup. The two are cross-checked: if they disagree on an amount, the one that's physically plausible wins (the EMI must cover the interest; outstanding can't exceed the original loan).

  3. 3
    You review, then add

    A form shows what was read. "Years left" is derived from the EMI on the statement, and a green tick confirms the EMI matches the terms — an amber warning means something was misread; adjust and the tick returns. Nothing is saved until you tap Add loan. "Text that was read" shows the raw OCR if you want to check.

Under the hood

How the maths works

Everything is computed on your phone from the formulas below. Nothing is fetched.

All-in cost

Agreement value = base cost + floor rise + PLC.
Statutory levies = stamp duty (state rate × AV) + registration (1% of AV, capped at ₹30,000 in Maharashtra) + GST (rate × AV).
All-in cost = AV + statutory levies + builder extras + loan charges.
Day-one cash = AV × down payment % + statutory levies + builder extras + loan charges.

Pre-EMI during construction

Each month before handover: 62% of the sanctioned loan × monthly rate. Cash before handover = the sum of those months plus property tax for the period. No rent is assumed until handover.

The loan

EMI = P·r·(1+r)ᴺ ÷ ((1+r)ᴺ − 1), with r the monthly rate and N the months.
Step-up: EMI in year y = base EMI × (1 + step-up)^(y−1).
Prepayments reduce principal in the month entered. MaxGain: interest each month accrues on max(0, balance − parked balance).

Tax

Net annual value = rent × 12 × (1 − vacancy) − property tax.
Section 24(a): 30% of NAV is deductible.
Section 24(b): interest is deductible up to ₹2 L (₹4 L if joint).
Taxable rent = max(0, NAV − 24(a) − 24(b)), taxed at your slab.
Monthly cash flow = rent − EMI − property tax − tax.

Return and break-even

Return per year is the XIRR of: −day-one cash, then each month's cash flow (lump sums included), then in month 120 the flat's value at your growth rate minus the loan still outstanding.
Break-even is the first month where value − loan obligation + cumulative cash flow ≥ 0. Before handover the obligation is the full sanctioned loan, not just the part drawn, because the rest is still owed to the builder.

Benchmarks

The same contribution stream — day-one cash and every monthly outflow — is grown at 12% (Nifty, then 12.5% LTCG on the gain), 7.8% (REIT) and 8.5% (prepaying debt). Their annualized figures are the XIRR of that stream.

The verdict rules

CallRule
BuyReturn ≥ the Nifty SIP figure, and cash before handover ≤ 30% of day-one cash
MarginalReturn ≥ 8.5% but below the Nifty SIP — or Buy on return but a heavy pre-handover cash burden

FAQ

Why is my yield red when it's a positive number?

Because it's judged against your loan rate. A 3% yield on money borrowed at 8.5% means the flat costs you 5.5% a year to hold, before price growth. Red is the honest colour.

The verdict says Rent + SIP. Is the app anti-property?

No — it's anti-assumption. Set price growth to 9% and most Indian metro deals turn Marginal or Buy. The point of the three scenarios is to show you exactly how much of the decision is riding on that number.

Does "Cash before handover" include my rent elsewhere?

No. It's the pre-EMI interest plus property tax on the new flat. Whatever you pay in rent to live somewhere meanwhile is on top.

Why does the loan curve jump up at handover?

During construction the bank disburses progressively (modelled at 62% on average). At handover the full sanctioned amount is outstanding and the real EMI begins.

My state isn't Maharashtra, Karnataka or Delhi.

Choose Other (6% stamp duty, 1% registration) and adjust mentally, or tell me your state's schedule and I'll add it.

Is my data backed up?

Deals are stored on the device. There's no cloud sync yet, so deleting the app deletes your deals. Share the verdict card to keep a record.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's a model with visible assumptions. Talk to your CA, and read the agreement.
